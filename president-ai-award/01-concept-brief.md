# Xotira — Concept Brief

**An AI memory wearable for people who are losing theirs.**

---

## The problem

Uzbekistan's population is aging. Tens of thousands of families care for elderly
relatives with age-related memory decline, mild cognitive impairment, and
dementia/Alzheimer's. For these patients, everyday life quietly falls apart:

- They forget whether they took their medication — and take it twice, or not at all.
- They forget what the doctor told them minutes after leaving the clinic.
- They forget names, faces, and conversations, which is frightening and isolating.
- Caregivers (usually family) have no reliable window into the patient's day.

Today the "solutions" are paper notes, sticky reminders, and exhausted relatives.
Nothing *understands* the patient's day and lets them ask about it.

## The solution — Xotira

A lightweight wrist wearable (bracelet/watch form factor) paired with a phone app.
Xotira captures the wearer's day with consent, understands it with AI, and turns it
into a searchable memory the patient and caregiver can rely on.

**Four core capabilities**

1. **Remember** — captures spoken moments (and, optionally, scenes) throughout the day.
2. **Understand** — transcribes Uzbek/Russian/English speech, identifies who spoke,
   and structures the day into events, people, instructions, and reminders.
3. **Recall** — the wearer (or caregiver) asks in plain language: *"Did I take my
   morning pills?"*, *"What did Dr. Karimova say about my blood pressure?"*,
   *"Who was the man who visited today?"* — Xotira answers from the day's memory.
4. **Reassure** — a gentle daily summary goes to a trusted caregiver, plus alerts
   for missed medication or unusual patterns.

## Why this is an AI product (not a voice recorder)

- **Speech-to-text** tuned for Uzbek + Russian + English, including code-switching.
- **Speaker diarization** — knows the patient from the doctor from a family member.
- **Semantic memory** — every moment is embedded and stored in a vector memory, so
  recall works by *meaning*, not keywords.
- **LLM retrieval (RAG)** — natural-language questions are answered by reasoning
  over the day's structured memory, with sources.
- **On-device intelligence** — wake/consent detection and privacy filtering run
  locally before anything leaves the device.

## Who it helps (in priority order)

1. **Elderly people with memory decline / early dementia** — dignity and independence.
2. **Their caregivers and families** — peace of mind, a daily window, fewer emergencies.
3. **Doctors & clinics** — the "consultation companion" captures and summarizes
   visits so instructions aren't lost.
4. **Later:** blind / low-vision users (scene description, reading aloud).

## Privacy by design (this is the whole ballgame)

A device that hears everything only earns trust if privacy is the foundation:

- **Consent + visible indicator** when capturing; simple one-tap pause.
- **On-device pre-filtering** — sensitive audio is processed locally; only what's
  needed leaves the device.
- **Encryption** in transit and at rest.
- **Data residency in Uzbekistan** — memory stays in-country.
- **User & caregiver control** — view, export, and permanently delete any memory.
- **Consent for third parties** — a clear tone/indicator so others know.

## Market (Uzbekistan first)

- **Beachhead:** families of elderly patients with memory decline in Tashkent +
  major cities; partnerships with neurology/geriatric clinics.
- **Expansion:** nationwide elderly care, then Central Asia (shared languages,
  aging demographics), then export as an Uzbek AI-hardware success story.
- **Model:** device + subscription (families) and B2B pilots (clinics, care homes).
